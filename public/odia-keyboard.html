How the spelling works

  1. ka and k both give କ — a consonant keeps its inherent a, the way the word is written.
  2. A vowel after a consonant becomes its matra: ki କି, kii କୀ, ko କୋ.
  3. Two consonants in a row take the halanta between them: swara ସ୍ୱର.
  4. Capitals are the retroflex and long letters: T D N S L R ଟ ଡ ଣ ଷ ଳ ଡ଼, A I U ଆ ଈ ଊ.
  5. So a doubled letter stays a geminate: uttara ଉତ୍ତର, never ଉଟର.
  6. _ forces a bare halanta (rum_ ରୁମ୍); M H ~ are anusvara, visarga, candrabindu; | is the danda.
  7. Anything unrecognised — spaces, Latin punctuation, text already in Odia — passes through untouched.
Odia letters are round because of the palm leaf. Scribes cut the script into ତାଳପତ୍ର with an iron stylus, and a straight stroke splits the leaf along its fibre — so the strokes curve, and every letter grew the umbrella head it still carries. The writing surface above is ruled the same way.
